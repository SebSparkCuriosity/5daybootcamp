Keep this beside you. Glance at it, do not read it out. You are aiming to talk less than a third of the time.

## Open (about one minute)
- Thank them for the time and name how long you will take (aim for 25 to 30 minutes).
- Ask consent to record: "Is it OK if I record this so I can listen properly rather than scribble? I will not share it." Wait for a clear yes. If no, take written notes only.
- Set expectations: "I am not selling anything today. I am trying to understand how you do [the job], nothing more."

## Questions

Write eight or more. Every one asks about the past or their current behaviour. If a question can be answered "yes" or "sounds good", rewrite it. Group them like this.

### How they do the job today
1. Walk me through how you handle [the job] at the moment, start to finish.
2. [ADD ONE about the tools or people involved in that process today.]

### The last time the problem bit
3. Tell me about the last time [the problem] came up. What did you do?
4. How long did that take you, and what did it cost, in time or money?

### What they have already tried or paid for
5. What are you using to deal with this today, and what does it cost?
6. Have you ever tried to fix this before? What happened?

### Who else is involved
7. Who else touches this decision or feels this problem alongside you?
8. If you wanted to change how this is done, whose sign-off would you need?

### Aimed at the riskiest assumption (write at least two)
9. [ADD a specific, past-tense question that proves or kills your riskiest assumption.]
10. [ADD a second one, from a different angle.]

## Close (about two minutes)
- "Who else doing your kind of job should I be speaking to?" (This is how you fill your list.)
- "Would it be alright if I came back to you once I have spoken to a few more people?"
- Thank them again. Stop the recording.

## The one rule, on the page
Opinions are worthless. Facts about the past are gold. Ask only questions that cannot be flattered.
