# Task and probe bank

Starting points you adapt. Do not paste them raw. The wording that fits your idea
and your business path is always better than a generic line.

## Writing good tasks

A task is something to attempt, not an opinion to give. Compare:

- Weak: "What do you think of the sign-up form?"
- Strong: "Book a 30-minute valuation for next Tuesday. Talk me through what you are doing as you go."

Rules:
- Start every task at a real point on the live URL.
- Give the goal, not the steps. If you tell them where to click, you learn nothing.
- Write one observable success condition per task, the same acceptance criterion from your story map.
- Order tasks the way a real user moves through the backbone.

## The three buying-signal probes

Three, no more. Each should cost the tester something to answer, because comfort answers
are worthless. Adapt the wording, keep the intent.

### 1. Price reaction
You want a flinch or a nod, not a shrug. Name a real number.

- Software: "This would be £X a month per user. What is your gut reaction, honestly?"
- Hardware: "The pre-order price is £X, shipping in [month]. Would you put a deposit down today?"
- Services: "The package is £X, delivered in [N] days. Where does that sit for you, cheap, fair, or steep?"

Listen for the reason behind the number, not the number itself.

### 2. Switch cost
Reveals whether the pain beats the effort of change.

- "What are you using for this today, and what would it take for you to drop it?"
- "Who else would have to say yes before your firm could use this?"

### 3. Commitment (a small real action now)
The truest signal. Ask for something small and real, in the session.

- Software: "Can I set up your account now while you are here?" (watch if they hesitate)
- Hardware: "Shall I add you to the pre-order list with your email?"
- Services: "Want me to pencil in the intake call for next week?"

A yes here outweighs any amount of warm praise. A "let me think about it" is data too:
note exactly what they wanted to think about, because that is the real objection.

## What you are listening for

- First hesitation on each task (worst friction).
- The gap between what they say and what they do. Watch the hands, not the mouth.
- Any moment they reach for their own wording for the problem. That wording is your Day 5 sales copy.
