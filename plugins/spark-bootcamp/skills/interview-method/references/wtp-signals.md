# Willingness-to-pay signals

The hardest thing to read in any interview is whether the person actually wants the thing enough to pay for it. Everyone is nice. Niceness is free, so it means nothing. The signal you are hunting for is commitment: the moment someone gives up something real. Money, time, reputation, a concrete next step. That costs them, so it is honest. Learn to hear the difference and your synthesis stops flattering you.

## Signal versus noise
Compliments feel like progress and are not. Commitment feels awkward to ask for and is the only thing that counts. Here is the split.

| Noise (feels good, means nothing) | Signal (costs them, means something) |
|---|---|
| "That is a great idea." | "Can you build it by next month? I need it." |
| "I would definitely use that." | "Here is my card, email me when it is ready." |
| "This is really clever." | "Let me introduce you to the person who holds the budget." |
| "Everyone I know has this problem." | "I currently pay £X a month for a tool that does half of this." |
| "Keep me posted." | "Can I pre-order? What is the deposit?" |
| "I love the design." | "I blocked out an hour to walk you through our workflow." |

The pattern: noise is about your idea and their feelings. Signal is about their money, their time, their name, and a specific next action.

## The two currencies that matter
Two things prove real interest, and they are the currencies to listen for.

**Commitment** is giving up something now. A deposit, a signed letter of intent, a paid pilot, a firm calendar slot, an introduction to their boss. The bigger the thing given up, the stronger the signal.

**Advance** is the relationship moving to a concrete next step with a date on it. "Send me a proposal by Friday and I will take it to the partners on Monday" is an advance. "Keep me posted" is not; it is a polite exit. If a conversation ends with no advance, treat it as a soft no however warm it felt.

## How to end a conversation to test the signal
Do not end on "thanks, that was helpful". End with an ask that costs them a little, sized to the path.

For software: "I am building a working version this week. Can I send you the link on Thursday and get ten minutes of you actually trying it?" A yes with a time is signal.

For hardware: "I am taking a small number of pre-orders with a refundable deposit. Would you want one?" A yes, or even a serious question about price and timing, is signal.

For services: "I can put together a sample of the deliverable for your case. If it is useful, would you book a paid first engagement?" A booked intake slot is signal.

If they dodge the ask, that is your answer, and it is more useful than any compliment. Note it honestly.

## Scoring a conversation
When synthesising, score each interview on behaviour, not warmth. A simple three-band read works:
- **Strong**: gave a commitment or a dated advance. Money, deposit, booked slot, introduction, or a firm next step.
- **Warm but unproven**: engaged, described real pain, but gave nothing up. Follow up once, do not count as demand yet.
- **Polite pass**: compliments only, no advance. Count as a no.

Ten "strong" reads is a business. Ten "polite pass" reads dressed up as enthusiasm is the trap this reference exists to prevent.

## The one line to remember
Watch what they give up, not what they say. Money, time, reputation and a dated next step are the only votes that count.
