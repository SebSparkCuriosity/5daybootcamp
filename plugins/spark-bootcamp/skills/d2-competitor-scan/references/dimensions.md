# Competitor dimensions: a menu by sector

Pick five or more. Choose the ones your buyer weighs when they decide, not the ones that make you look good. A dimension is only worth a column if a real buyer would ask about it.

## The core five (start here, works for any business)

1. **Price / price model.** Number if you can find it. "Price on request" is a data point: note it, it usually means slow and expensive to buy.
2. **Who it is built for.** The segment the rival actually serves. Often narrower than their marketing claims.
3. **Main strength.** The one thing they genuinely do well. Be fair; you learn nothing by pretending rivals are useless.
4. **Main weakness.** The gap the buyer complains about. Reviews and forums are gold here.
5. **The regulated-Jersey dimension.** Pick one that matters locally: data residency, audit trail, GDPR / Jersey DPA posture, or local support and accountability. Off-island tools often lose here.

## Extra dimensions by sector

### Finance, fund admin, trust, wealth
- Regulatory fit (JFSC awareness, audit trail, four-eyes / maker-checker support).
- Data residency and where the data is hosted.
- Integration with existing systems (Viewpoint, NavOne, FIS, core banking).
- Onboarding time and switching cost.

### Law and accountancy
- Confidentiality and privilege handling.
- Time to first useful output.
- Whether a human stays in the loop (matters for professional liability).
- Billing model fit (fixed vs time-recorded).

### Hospitality, construction, professional services
- Setup effort and training burden.
- Reliability and support responsiveness.
- Contract lock-in length.
- Total cost including hidden setup or per-seat fees.

## Software-specific
- Deployment (SaaS vs self-hosted).
- API and export (can the buyer get their data out?).
- Uptime / SLA if published.
- Review score (G2, Capterra) with count, cited.

## Hardware-specific
- Unit price and volume pricing.
- Lead time and stock availability.
- Build quality and materials.
- Warranty and returns.
- Certifications (CE, UKCA) where relevant.

## Services-specific
- Fixed price vs hourly.
- Turnaround time.
- Degree of productisation (packaged vs bespoke).
- Specialism depth vs generalist.
- Clarity of the offer (a vague rival is beatable on clarity alone).

## Sourcing rules
- Every non-obvious cell needs a source: a URL, a review link, a spec sheet, or a dated phone note.
- A guess is allowed only if labelled "assumption" in the cell, and it becomes something you test.
- Never state a rival's revenue, headcount or market share unless you can cite it. Fabricated market numbers break the Spark rule and undermine the whole scan.
