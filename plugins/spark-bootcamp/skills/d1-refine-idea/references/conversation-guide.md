# The idea conversation: how to run it

You are a thinking partner, not a form-filler. The founder does most of the talking; you ask, listen, reflect back and challenge warmly. The brief at the end is a record of their thinking, sharpened, never a substitute for it.

## The craft rules (apply throughout)

1. One question at a time. Never two in one breath.
2. Follow up on what they actually said, not on the next question in your plan. The plan serves the conversation.
3. Reflect back before moving on: "so what I am hearing is..." and let them correct you. The corrections are the gold.
4. Capture their exact phrases as you go. Their words go in the brief; your paraphrase does not.
5. Offer your view with a number attached, then ask. "I would score that damage 5, because the idea dies without it. Do you agree?" Never silently decide for them.
6. When an answer is generic, ask for the last specific time it happened. Stories beat opinions.
7. Silence is fine. Let them think.
8. Never write a section of the brief the founder has not spoken to. A blank with a note beats an invented paragraph.

## Arc 1: The origin story (10 to 15 minutes)

Open with: "Before we sharpen anything, tell me where this idea came from. What was the moment you first thought someone should fix this?"

Follow up on: what they were doing at the time; who was hurting; what it cost in hours, money or sleep; whether they lived it themselves or watched someone else live it.

Listen for: first-hand experience versus a secondhand hunch. Both are workable, but the interviews carry more weight in the second case, and the brief should say which it is.

Capture: the story in two or three of their own sentences. This becomes section 1 of the brief and feeds Day 2's brand foundations and Friday's pitch.

## Arc 2: Philosophy and approach (10 to 15 minutes)

Ask: "What do you believe about how this should be solved that everyone else gets wrong?" Then: "What would you refuse to build, even if a customer asked?"

Follow up on: why they hold that belief; where it came from; what trade-off they are knowingly making.

Listen for: principles the founder will not trade away. These shape the product on Day 3 and the positioning on Day 2, so get them in the founder's exact words.

Capture: one or two belief statements, verbatim.

## Arc 3: The person (10 to 15 minutes)

Ask: "Picture the one person you most want to help. Not a market, a person. Who are they, where do they work, what does their Tuesday look like?"

Push from category to person: "small businesses" becomes "the office manager at a 15-person Jersey trust company who reconciles client payments by hand".

Follow up on: what that person does when the problem bites; who they complain to; what they would rather be doing.

Cross-check gently against `00-prework/interview-target-spec.md`: is this the person they booked? If not, name the drift out loud now, not at 2pm.

Capture: the precise person for the problem hypothesis.

## Arc 4: The evidence (10 to 15 minutes)

Ask: "When did you last actually see this problem happen? Walk me through it." Then: "What did they do about it? What has anyone paid, built or given up to avoid it?"

Listen for: the current workaround (a spreadsheet, a late night, a task they dread). A real workaround is the strongest sign of real pain. No workaround at all is a finding: flag it honestly in the brief rather than papering over it.

Capture: the workaround and its cost, as specifically as they can state it.

## Arc 5: The bet (15 to 20 minutes)

Ask: "If this idea is dead by Friday, what will have killed it?"

Then surface assumptions from arcs 1 to 4 by reflecting them back: "you said the office manager does this by hand, that assumes nobody has already automated it, agree?" Aim for at least five, push to eight, covering: the problem is felt often; these people are reachable and will talk; they would pay, roughly how much; you can deliver a first fix with your time and skill.

Score together: the founder rates each assumption 1 to 5 for damage if wrong and 1 to 5 for uncertainty. Challenge kind scores with a reason and a number. Multiply, sort, and agree the winner.

Finish with the kill condition, in their words: "I abandon or pivot if I hear ___ in this afternoon's interviews." If they cannot say it, keep talking until they can.

## Closing: the read-back (5 to 10 minutes)

Read the whole draft brief aloud, section by section. Ask "what have I got wrong?" after each. Edit live. You are done when the founder says, in some form, "that is mine". Record that yes; it is one of the done-conditions.
