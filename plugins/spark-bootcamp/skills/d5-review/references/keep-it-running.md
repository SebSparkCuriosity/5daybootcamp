# Keep it running: the hand-over, in plain English

Claude built the MVP this week. That is not the same as the MVP running itself.
The moment a real customer relies on it, someone has to own the accounts, know
how to put a change live, and be honest about the limits. That someone is you.

This note explains the four things the keep-it-running section in
`LAUNCH-PACKAGE.md` asks you to fill in, and why each one matters. The section
itself is generated by `scripts/build_review.py`, branched to your business
path, with `[TO COMPLETE: ...]` markers wherever the script cannot know a fact
for certain (your account emails, your registrar, your refund policy). Fill
every marker. A blank marker is a thing that will bite you in week three.

## 1. Account ownership

If you cannot log in, you cannot keep it running. The single most common way a
founder loses a working product is a Vercel or Supabase account tied to an email
they no longer check, or a domain registered on someone else's card. List every
account, the provider, and the exact login that owns it. Do it today, while you
still remember which Google account you clicked "Sign in with".

For software the accounts are usually GitHub, Vercel, Supabase, a domain
registrar and an email or DNS provider. For hardware they are the CAD tool, the
payment processor holding deposits (Stripe), the waitlist data store and the
site host. For services they are the booking tool, the payment processor, the
place the sample deliverable template lives, and the site host.

## 2. How to put a change live

Write the redeploy steps as if you will follow them at 8am with a customer
waiting, because one day you will.

Software default stack: push to `main`, Vercel rebuilds and deploys in about 2
minutes. To force a deploy with no code change, run `vercel --prod` or press
Redeploy in the dashboard. Database changes go through a Supabase migration, not
a hand edit in the table editor, so every change is logged and repeatable.

Hardware: how to update the pre-order page and the deposit amount in Stripe, and
how to export the deposit list. Services: how to update the intake page and
change your availability in the booking tool, in one place, not by email.

## 3. Known limits

Say what could stop it working before a customer finds out the hard way. Free
tiers have caps: a Supabase free project pauses after 7 days of no traffic, and
a Vercel hobby project is non-commercial, so budget roughly £20 to £25 a month
each before a real customer depends on it. There is one environment, so a change
goes straight to the customer. There are no database backups unless you turned
them on. Name the third-party quotas and any secrets that expire.

## 4. What you are committing to the customer

This is the honest one, and the one founders skip. A customer who pays you
believes something about support, even if you never said it. So say it, with
numbers: how fast you reply, how fast you fix a breakage, what is in scope and
what is not, the one channel they reach you on, and the date you both review
whether to continue. A vague promise you cannot keep is worse than a small
promise you can. Under-promise the response time, then beat it.

Keep this short. Six lines with numbers beats a page of reassurance.
