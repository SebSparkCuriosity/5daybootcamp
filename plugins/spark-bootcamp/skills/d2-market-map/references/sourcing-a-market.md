# Finding the real players in twenty minutes

You will almost never have "no competition". You have competition you have not
found yet. Here is where to look, fastest first. Every player you find gets a
source recorded next to its name. No source, no entry.

## Start with your own interviews
The quickest source is already in your pocket. Go back through your Day 1 notes.
Every time an interviewee said "at the moment we use...", "we tried...", or "we
pay someone to...", that is an incumbent or a substitute. Name it, source it as
"named by interviewee [initials], Day 1". These are the best entries on the map
because a real buyer told you they use them.

## Jersey and Channel Islands specifics
- **Jersey Financial Services Commission register** for regulated firms in
  finance, fund admin, trust and wealth. If your buyers are regulated, their
  suppliers often are too.
- **Jersey Business** and **Digital Jersey** member directories for local
  providers and tech firms.
- **Jersey Companies Registry** to confirm a competitor is a real trading
  entity and roughly how long it has existed.
- Local trade bodies: the **Jersey Law Society** for advocates and solicitors,
  the relevant accountancy and fund associations for those sectors.

## UK and global
- A plain web search for "[the job] software" and "[the job] service [Jersey/UK]".
  Read the ads as well as the results: whoever is paying to appear is a serious
  incumbent.
- **G2** and **Capterra** for software categories, to find the global SaaS
  players in the "big generic tool" bucket.
- **LinkedIn** company search for the segment plus the job, to find firms that
  quietly offer it as a service.
- **Companies House** (UK) to confirm a UK competitor is real.

## The three buckets, so you do not miss anyone
- **Direct**: solves the same problem the same way. Easy to find, and the ones
  founders overweight.
- **Adjacent**: solves a neighbouring problem and could swing into yours with
  one feature or one hire. Easy to miss, often the real threat.
- **DIY / big generic tool**: the spreadsheet, the manual process, the free tool,
  the global SaaS nobody loves. Always populated. This is where most of the
  "market" actually sits, so never leave it empty.

## The honesty test
If after an honest hour across your interviews, the JFSC register, a web search
and G2 you have found fewer than 6 players, widen the definition of the problem,
not your imagination. Ask: "what does the customer pay for, or spend time on,
to make this problem smaller today?" There is always an answer, even if the
answer is "a person and a spreadsheet".
