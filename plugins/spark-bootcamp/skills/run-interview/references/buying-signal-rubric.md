# Buying-signal rubric (product-test mode)

Score every product-test session 0 to 5. The rule that matters: score what the person DID, not what they said. Commitment is giving up something real. A compliment costs nothing, so it counts for nothing.

## The scale

| Score | Name | What earns it | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Polite | Compliments only, no action offered | You learned nothing about demand |
| 1 | Interest | Asked to be kept posted, gave nothing | Curiosity, not intent |
| 2 | Time | Agreed a specific follow-up, or tried the thing properly on their own steam | They will spend minutes on you |
| 3 | Reputation | Gave a warm introduction, or their name as a reference | They will stake a little social capital |
| 4 | Intent | Signed a waitlist, pre-order or letter of intent with real details (name, company, email they check) | A promise with their identity attached |
| 5 | Money | Paid, put down a deposit, or committed a purchase order now | The only signal that fully counts |

## How to score honestly
Pick the highest row they actually reached through action. If a tester says "definitely send me the pre-order link" but does not sign it in front of you, that is a 1, not a 4. The gap between what people say and what they do is exactly what this score measures.

Write one line of evidence next to the number: the specific thing they did. "Signed the pre-order form with work email at 14:32" is evidence. "Seemed keen" is not.

## What the week needs
The sprint target is a first paying customer, so a single genuine 5 is worth more than ten 2s. If you are stacking up 0s and 1s, the problem is usually one of three things: the wrong person (not a real buyer), the wrong pain (mild, not urgent), or an ask that was too soft to test anything. Change one and run the next session.
