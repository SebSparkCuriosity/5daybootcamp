# Discovery prompt sheet (keep open during the call)

This is your live companion for a discovery interview. Glance at it, do not read it out. The whole aim is to hear about the past in specifics and never to pitch. If you catch yourself explaining your idea, stop and ask another question about what actually happened.

## First 60 seconds
Read the consent block, log the four fields, then get out of the way. Say who you are in one sentence and that there are no wrong answers. Do not describe your product.

## The opener (get them talking about the real situation)
- "Walk me through the last time you had to deal with [the problem area]."
- "When did that last come up? What were you doing right before?"

## Dig into what happened (Mom Test rule: past and specific, never hypothetical)
- "What did you do about it?"
- "How long did that take? What did it cost you?"
- "Who else was involved?"
- "What did you try before that?"
- "What happened next?"

Follow every general claim with "tell me about the last time". A generic answer ("we always struggle with X") is worth nothing until it is one real event.

## Read the job (why this matters to them)
- "What were you actually trying to get done?"
- "What does good look like when it goes right?"
- "What do you use for this today?"
- "Where does that break down?"

## Test for real cost (is this pain or mild annoyance?)
- "How often does this happen?"
- "Have you spent money trying to fix it? On what?"
- "If it never got fixed, what would that mean for you?"

## Traps to avoid
- Do not ask "would you use this?" or "would you pay for this?". People are kind and wrong about the future.
- Do not pitch. The moment you pitch, the truth stops.
- Do not fish for compliments. A compliment is the polite sound a conversation makes when nothing useful is happening.

## Close with an ask that tests commitment
- "Who else should I be talking to?"
- "Can I come back to you when I have something to show?"
Note what they actually agree to. A real yes is a diary date or a name, not "sure, sounds good".

## While you talk, capture
Write down their exact words in quote marks. You need three or more verbatim quotes in the record. Do not paraphrase in the moment; paraphrase later, quote now.
