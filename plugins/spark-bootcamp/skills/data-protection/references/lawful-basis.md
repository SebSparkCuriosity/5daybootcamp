Prepared by {{BUSINESS_NAME}} ({{FOUNDER}}) on {{DATE}}. This is your internal working note: for every kind of personal data you touch this week, it records the reason the law lets you process it. You are not required to publish it, but if a regulator or a client asks "on what basis?", this is the answer, written down before you needed it.

The Data Protection (Jersey) Law 2018 and the UK GDPR each require a lawful basis for processing personal data. The common ones for a firm at this stage are: **consent**, **contract** (processing needed to take steps at someone's request before, or to perform, a contract), and **legitimate interests** (a real need of yours that does not override the person's rights). Special-category data (health, beliefs, and so on) needs an extra condition, so avoid collecting it unless you truly must.

## Data minimisation
Collect the least you need. Every extra field is a liability you have to protect, justify and delete. If you cannot name the lawful basis for a field, do not collect it.
