# Naming checklist

A name does one job: it lets the right person remember you and find you again. It does not need to be clever. It needs to be sayable, spellable and available. Resolve it here, or set a clear direction and a shortlist of at most 3. Do not spend the whole week naming.

## First, decide the mode
- **Keep** your working name. Fine if it already passes the five gates below. Record that it does.
- **Refine** it. Small change, a word dropped or added.
- **Choose fresh.** Only if the working name fails a gate you cannot live with.

## The five gates
Run every candidate through all five. A name that fails gate 1 or 2 is out, no matter how nice it looks.

1. **Says something true.** It hints at what you do or how you feel to work with, or it is a clean blank you can fill with meaning. It does not promise something false.
2. **Easy to say aloud.** You can say it on a phone call without spelling it. If people stumble, they will not repeat it.
3. **Spellable after hearing it once.** No silent letters, no "is that with a k or a c". A name people cannot type is a name they cannot find.
4. **A domain is gettable.** The .com or .je is free, or a close, clean variant is (getname, name-app, trythename). Flag this as "confirm before spending", buying a domain is a Day 4 money decision.
5. **Not already taken in your sector on the island.** A quick search shows no firm trading under it in your space in Jersey, Guernsey or the UK. Flag a proper trademark check as "confirm before spending".

## Generating candidates, if choosing fresh
Aim for 8 to 10 candidates, then cut to a shortlist of 3.
- **Descriptive:** says the category (Jersey Fund Reports).
- **Compound:** two plain words joined (ClearTrust, SwiftClose).
- **Coined:** an invented, ownable word (short, easy to say).
- **Metaphor:** a word that carries the feeling (Anchor, Beacon, Ledger).

## The tie-breaker
When two names survive, pick the one you would least mind saying out loud fifty times a week for two years. Then write one line in DECISIONS.md: the name you chose and why the others lost.

## Before you commit
Do a quick web search for obvious clashes and any unfortunate meaning in another language. This is a five-minute sanity check, not a legal clearance. The full domain purchase and trademark search happen later, with sign-off, when money is involved.
