# Tone of voice: the 4 dimensions

Tone is not vague. Rate yourself 1 to 5 on each of these four sliders and your voice becomes a set of decisions anyone can follow. Note the number and one line on why, then write one example sentence that proves it.

## 1. Formal (1) to Casual (5)
How much distance you keep from the reader.
- 1 to 2: "We would be glad to discuss your requirements." Suits trust, law, private wealth.
- 3: "Happy to talk it through whenever suits." A professional but human middle.
- 4 to 5: "Give us a shout and we will sort it." Suits hospitality, trades, challenger brands.

## 2. Serious (1) to Playful (5)
How much wit and lightness you allow.
- 1 to 2: straight, measured, no jokes. Right for high-stakes or regulated work.
- 3: warm, the occasional light touch.
- 4 to 5: humour is part of the brand. Right where the buyer wants to enjoy the experience.

## 3. Plain (1) to Expressive (5)
How much colour and metaphor in the language.
- 1 to 2: short words, no flourish, every sentence earns its place. Spark's own default.
- 3: mostly plain, the odd vivid phrase.
- 4 to 5: rich, image-led, memorable turns of phrase. Right for lifestyle and design-led brands.

## 4. Reserved (1) to Bold (5)
How strong a claim you are willing to make.
- 1 to 2: cautious, caveated, understated.
- 3: confident but measured.
- 4 to 5: makes the strong claim and stands behind it. "We will halve your onboarding time."

## Reading the result

Most good brands are not 3, 3, 3, 3. That is beige. Pick a shape.
- A sharp regulated challenger might be 2 formal, 2 playful, 1 plain, 4 bold: serious and understated in style, but making a strong, specific claim. That is a strong, distinctive voice inside strict bounds.
- A beach cafe might be 4, 4, 4, 4: casual, playful, expressive, bold.

## The example sentence

After you rate the sliders, write one real sentence in that voice, for example the opening line of a cold email or the hero line of your landing page. If the sentence does not match the numbers, one of them is wrong. Fix it now, before it spreads into every document you write this week.
