# The 12 brand archetypes

Pick exactly one. An archetype is a shorthand for how you want the buyer to feel about you. It keeps your tone, your imagery and your promises pointing the same way. Choosing two is choosing none.

Below are the 12 recognised archetypes, each with what it stands for and a one-line example so you can see the fit. Read them once, then pick the single closest match to the buyer in your proposition.

| Archetype | Stands for | Feels like | Example fit |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| The Sage | Truth, expertise, clarity | "They know, and they will tell me straight." | A fund-admin advisory that explains the rules plainly |
| The Hero | Mastery, courage, results | "They will get it done." | A firm that fixes the thing everyone else gave up on |
| The Creator | Imagination, craft, originality | "They make things that did not exist." | A product studio, a bespoke maker |
| The Ruler | Order, control, quality | "They set the standard." | A premium wealth or private-office service |
| The Caregiver | Service, protection, reassurance | "They have got me covered." | Compliance, trust services, anything that removes worry |
| The Everyman | Belonging, honesty, no airs | "They are one of us." | A no-nonsense local trades or hospitality business |
| The Explorer | Freedom, discovery, independence | "They help me find my own way." | A tool that opens up options |
| The Outlaw | Disruption, rule-breaking, change | "They do it differently on purpose." | A challenger taking on a stale incumbent |
| The Magician | Transformation, vision, the "wow" | "They make the hard thing feel effortless." | Automation that turns a week of work into an hour |
| The Lover | Intimacy, taste, closeness | "They get the detail right." | A high-touch, design-led service or venue |
| The Jester | Fun, lightness, wit | "They make this enjoyable." | A brand that refuses to be boring |
| The Innocent | Simplicity, optimism, trust | "They keep it clean and honest." | A brand built on doing one simple thing well |

## How to choose

1. Read the proposition. What does the buyer most want to feel: safe, impressed, understood, free, delighted?
2. Match that feeling to one archetype above.
3. Sanity-check against your values. If your top value is "we ship in weeks" and you picked the Ruler, you might really be the Hero. Adjust.
4. Write one sentence: "We are the [archetype] because our buyer wants to feel [feeling] and we deliver it by [what you do]."

## For regulated Jersey sectors

Finance, trust, fund admin, law and accountancy buyers usually respond to the Sage, the Caregiver or the Ruler. That does not mean you must pick one of those. A well-judged Magician (effortless transformation) or Hero (gets it done) can stand out in a sea of grey Sages, as long as your tone stays inside what a regulator would nod at. Distinctive beats safe, but reckless loses the client.
