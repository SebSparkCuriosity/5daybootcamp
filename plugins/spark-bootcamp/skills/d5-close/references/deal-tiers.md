# The four win tiers, ranked

A win is a win. But not all wins are equal, and you should always reach for the highest tier
the conversation will bear before you drop to the next. Ask for cash. If they hesitate, ask for
a deposit. If a deposit is a stretch, sell a paid pilot. If none of those land today but the
intent is real, get it in writing with a date and an amount. All four count as the week's win.

Rank them in this order and stop climbing down the moment one lands.

## Tier 1: cash in full

The whole invoice paid before you start. Best outcome. Most common for services under £2,000 and
for software subscriptions billed up front. Proof: money in the account, or a paid Stripe receipt.

**Ask like this:** "The package is £X. I'll send the invoice now and we start Monday. Card or
transfer?"

## Tier 2: a paid deposit

Part of the fee paid now, the rest on a named milestone. Standard for larger services engagements
and for hardware pre-orders. Aim for 30 to 50 percent up front. Proof: the deposit cleared, and a
one-line schedule for the balance.

**Ask like this:** "It's £X total. Let's take £Y today to hold the slot and lock the price, and
the balance when we ship. That work?"

## Tier 3: a signed paid pilot

A small, time-boxed, paid trial that converts to the full engagement if it hits a target. Right
when the buyer believes but needs to see it work once. Keep it short (two to four weeks) and price
it so it stings a little (£500 minimum). A free pilot is not a win. Proof: signed pilot terms plus
the pilot fee paid or invoiced.

**Ask like this:** "Let's prove it. A two-week paid pilot at £X. If it hits [the number], we roll
into the full package. If it doesn't, we stop. Fair?"

## Tier 4: a signed letter of intent, with a date and an amount

A written commitment to buy, naming what, how much, and by when. The floor. Use it only when
money genuinely cannot move today (procurement, board sign-off, a budget that opens next quarter).
It must carry a specific amount and a specific start date, and the buyer must sign it. A vague
"yes, let's do something soon" is not a win. Proof: a signed LOI with £ and a date.

**Ask like this:** "I understand the money moves in [month]. Let's put it in writing now: the
package, the price, and a start date, so we both know it's real. I'll send it today for your
signature."

## The one rule

Never leave the room without a tier. If you find yourself at "let me think about it", you have
not asked clearly enough. Restate the package, restate the number, and ask for the lowest tier
that is still a real commitment. Silence after the ask is normal. Let them fill it.
