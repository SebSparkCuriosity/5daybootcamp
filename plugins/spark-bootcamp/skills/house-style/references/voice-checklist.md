# Spark Voice Checklist

Run this over every piece of prose before you save it. Work top to bottom. The first two are hard rules: a single failure means the artefact is not finished. The rest are tone rules: aim to pass all of them, fix the worst offenders first.

## Hard rules (zero tolerance)

1. **No em dashes. Anywhere.** Search the text for the em dash character and the en dash character. Both must return zero. Replace with a comma, a colon, a full stop or brackets. This is the single most common way a document stops looking like Spark.
2. **British English throughout.** These words must use the British form: organise, organisation, recognise, behaviour, favour, favourite, prioritise, analyse, licence (noun), programme, colour, centre, defence, catalogue, cancelled, labelled, modelled. Scan for the American form of each and fix it. American spelling in a document sent to a Jersey trust or law firm reads as imported and generic.

## Tone rules

3. **Open with the conclusion.** The first sentence states the claim, the number or the recommendation. No throat-clearing, no "in today's landscape", no scene-setting paragraph before the point.
4. **One recommendation, not a menu.** Say what to do. "Do X" beats "you could consider X, Y or Z". Disagreement is allowed; hedging is not.
5. **Put a number on it.** Every recommendation carries a numeric or observable outcome. If a claim has no number and could have one, add it. If a figure is not sourced, flag it as an assumption in the text, never invent one.
6. **Pragmatic over impressive.** "Does it actually work?" beats "is it cutting-edge?". Cut any sentence that sells novelty instead of a result.
7. **Warm and first-name.** Friendly, direct, second person. Short sentences. No corporate-speak, no passive throat-clearing, no "we are delighted to".
8. **Vary the sentence length.** Short, declarative, structurally varied within a paragraph. Use a deliberate fragment now and then. End on the load-bearing word.
9. **Prose over bullets.** Default to paragraphs. Use a list only where a genuine comparison or a reference set needs structure.

## Phrases we never write

Reject and rewrite any of these on sight: "in today's fast-paced landscape", "we are delighted to", "leverage synergies", "cutting-edge", "seamless", "revolutionary", "game-changing", "at the end of the day", "reach out", "circle back", "best-in-class", "transformation journey", "unlock value".

## Phrases that sound like us

"Jersey is ready, but stuck." "The tools are the easy bit." "Pick the smallest painful workflow first." "Build for auditability from day one." "We put a number on it or we do not take the work."

## Quick self-check before saving

- Em dash count: 0.
- American spelling count on the listed words: 0.
- First sentence states the point: yes.
- At least one number in any section that makes a recommendation: yes.
- Any figure without a source is labelled an assumption: yes.
