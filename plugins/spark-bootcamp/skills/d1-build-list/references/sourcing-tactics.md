# Where to find 20+ of the right people, fast

Work these buckets top to bottom. The warm ones sit at the top on purpose. Empty them before you spend energy on cold outreach, because a warm name replies four to five times more often than a cold one.

## Start warm (do these first)

1. Your own phone and email. Scroll your contacts and sent mail. You almost certainly already know two or three people in or next to the segment.
2. Past colleagues and clients. Anyone you have worked with who now sits in the segment, or who manages people who do.
3. LinkedIn first connections. Filter by location (Jersey, Guernsey, Isle of Man) and by title. First connections are warm by definition.
4. Your accountant, lawyer, banker and insurer. Professional advisers in a small island know everyone in the regulated sectors and give introductions readily. Ask each for two names.

## Jersey and Channel Islands sources

5. Industry bodies and their member directories. Jersey Finance, the Jersey Funds Association, the Society of Trust and Estate Practitioners (STEP) Jersey, the Jersey Law Society, the Jersey Society of Chartered and Certified Accountants, the Jersey Construction Council and the Jersey Hospitality Association all publish or share member lists. The directory is your shortlist.
6. Digital Jersey. Its events, member list and community are a fast route to operators who are already open to trying new tools.
7. Networking and events. IoD Jersey, Chamber of Commerce Jersey, and sector breakfasts and mixers. Recent attendee or speaker lists are gold: these people show up to talk, so they will talk to you.
8. The parish and personal network. Jersey runs on introductions. A single well-connected person can hand you five names. Ask directly: "who are the two people I should be speaking to about this?"
9. Guernsey and the Isle of Man. Same sectors, same bodies (Guernsey Finance, We Are Guernsey, Digital Isle of Man). Widen here before you widen the UK.

## Cold, but targeted (last)

10. Company websites and LinkedIn company pages. Find the exact role at named firms and note the best contact route. Do not mass-message: pick specific people who clearly fit the segment.
11. Public registers and trade press. Business news, appointment notices and award shortlists name the people running things in each sector.

## Two rules while you hunt

Screen as you go. Every name must clear the three screening questions from your target spec. An easy-to-find name who fails the screen is a trap, not a win.

Record the source in the CSV. When Day 2 outreach starts, knowing a name came from a warm intro versus a cold directory changes how you approach them, and it tells you which sources are worth mining again if you fall short.
