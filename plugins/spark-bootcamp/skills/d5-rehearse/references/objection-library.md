# Objection library

The eight objections almost every Jersey buyer raises, with model answers in the
acknowledge / reframe / redirect shape. Rewrite them in your own words. These are
scaffolding, not a script to read.

The shape, every time:
- **Acknowledge** in one line. Do not fight the objection.
- **Reframe** with the number or the proof.
- **Redirect** with a question that moves forward.

A price objection is usually a value question in disguise. Answer the value, not the number.

---

## 1. "It's too expensive."
- Acknowledge: "Fair, it's real money."
- Reframe: "The package is £2,000. It saves the person doing this job roughly a day a week. At your cost of that time, it pays for itself inside the first month."
- Redirect: "What would it need to save you for the price to be obviously worth it?"

## 2. "I need to think about it."
- Acknowledge: "Of course, it's your call."
- Reframe: "Usually 'think about it' means one thing isn't clear yet."
- Redirect: "What's the one thing you'd want to be sure of before we start?"

## 3. "We already do this another way / with someone else."
- Acknowledge: "Good, so the need is real."
- Reframe: "This isn't about ripping that out. It's the smallest painful bit, done in a week, fixed price."
- Redirect: "Which part of your current way annoys you most?"

## 4. "Is it compliant / what about the regulator?"
- Acknowledge: "Right question to ask first in this sector."
- Reframe: "It's built auditable: a human signs off every step, every change is logged, and you get an audit pack. The regulator nods rather than flinches."
- Redirect: "Who on your side owns the compliance sign-off, so we build to their standard from day one?"

## 5. "What about our data / GDPR?"
- Acknowledge: "Non-negotiable, agreed."
- Reframe: "Your data stays yours. We document where it sits and who touches it, and the data-protection note is part of the deliverable."
- Redirect: "Shall I have our data-protection draft ready for your review before we touch anything live?"
- Note: any data-protection wording you hand over is a draft. Carry this line: "This is a draft. Have a qualified lawyer review it before you rely on it. Spark does not warrant it."

## 6. "Can you send me something / a proposal?"
- Acknowledge: "Happy to."
- Reframe: "You've already seen the proposal and the working slice. Paper rarely changes a decision the demo didn't."
- Redirect: "What's the specific thing the document needs to answer that I can just answer now?"

## 7. "Now isn't a good time."
- Acknowledge: "Timing matters, I get it."
- Reframe: "It's one to six weeks, not a big programme. It fits around a busy quarter rather than blocking one."
- Redirect: "When does this problem actually start costing you, and can we finish before then?"

## 8. "How do I know it'll work?"
- Acknowledge: "You shouldn't have to take it on faith."
- Reframe: "There's a numeric target we agree up front, and you can see the live page working before you commit."
- Redirect: "What number would you need to see move for this to be a clear success?"
