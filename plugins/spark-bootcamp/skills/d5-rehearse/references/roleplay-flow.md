# Role-play flow and scoring checklist

Claude plays the buyer. You play you. Run it once, start to close, without stopping.
Claude should throw at least 3 objections from your bank mid-flow, unannounced, the
way a real prospect does.

## The flow

1. **Open on their problem, not your product.** One sentence naming the pain they told
   you about. "You said month-end reconciliation eats two days. That's what this fixes."

2. **Discovery, three questions.** Confirm the pain is still live, confirm they own the
   decision, confirm roughly what it costs them today. Listen more than you talk.

3. **Show, don't tell.** Walk the live page, the render, or the sample deliverable,
   branched to your path. One minute. Point at the thing working, then stop.

4. **State the value as a number.** What they keep or earn, bigger than the fee.

5. **Name the price. Then stop talking.** Say it in under 8 seconds, end on a question,
   and let the silence sit. Do not fill it. Do not add "but we can be flexible".

6. **Handle whatever comes back** with acknowledge / reframe / redirect.

7. **Close.** The assumptive close first: "shall we start Monday?" If that stalls, the
   fallback close: a smaller yes. "Shall I send the intake link / take the deposit /
   turn on your account?"

## Scoring checklist

Score each run out of 6. Anything under 6 means run it again.

- [ ] Opened on their problem, in one sentence, before mentioning the product.
- [ ] Ran discovery and listened, rather than pitching over the top.
- [ ] Showed the working thing (live page / render / sample) for the right path.
- [ ] Named the price without hedging, under 8 seconds, ending on a question.
- [ ] Handled every objection with acknowledge, reframe, redirect. No arguing.
- [ ] Actually asked for the close. Out loud. And waited.

Two clean 6-out-of-6 runs beats one lucky one. The awkward silence after the price is
the whole point. If you can sit in it once, you can sit in it on the real call.
