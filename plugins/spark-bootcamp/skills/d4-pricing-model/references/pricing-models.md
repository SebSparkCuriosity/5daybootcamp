# The four pricing models

Read this before you choose. Each model is described plainly, with the one line that
tells you when it wins and the Jersey or regulated-sector note that matters most.

## 1. One-off build fee

You charge a single fixed price to build and hand over a thing. The customer pays once,
owns the result, and owes you nothing after.

- **Value metric.** Per system, per deliverable, per build. Flat.
- **When it wins.** The outcome is discrete and finishable, and the buyer wants to own it
  outright. Best for hardware prototypes and productised services. Fastest path to a real
  payment inside five days, because a fixed price with a fixed scope is easy to say yes to.
- **Watch out.** No recurring income. If you want a relationship, you have to sell again.
- **Jersey and regulated note.** A capital line item clears procurement faster than a new
  recurring subscription in trust, fund and law firms. It fits a budget approval, not a
  standing-cost review. This is often the quickest yes in these sectors.

## 2. Pay-for-access plus build-in-partnership

The customer pays to get in early and helps shape what you build. Part payment, part
design partner. Common for software still finding its shape.

- **Value metric.** Per seat or per early-access licence, sometimes plus a reduced build fee.
- **When it wins.** Software with a working slice but an unfinished product, and a buyer
  who wants influence over the roadmap. It funds the build and validates demand at once.
- **Watch out.** You owe the partner attention and changes. Cap the number of partners so
  you are not building four products.
- **Jersey and regulated note.** Regulated buyers like being a named design partner because
  it gives them control over compliance and audit behaviour. Offer that control explicitly.

## 3. Subscription

The customer pays a recurring fee (monthly or annual) for continued access or service.
Predictable income, higher lifetime value, slower first yes.

- **Value metric.** Per seat, per user, per unit of usage, per month.
- **When it wins.** Ongoing value that recurs, low marginal cost to serve, and a buyer who
  gets continuous benefit. Strong for software at scale. Weak inside a five-day sprint,
  because a new recurring cost usually needs sign-off you cannot get this week.
- **Watch out.** Slowest to a first payment. Churn punishes a weak product.
- **Jersey and regulated note.** A new standing cost often triggers a heavier approval than
  a one-off. Expect a longer cycle. Do not bank on closing a subscription in five days.

## 4. Retainer

The customer pays a fixed recurring fee for a defined bundle of your time or output each
period. Common for services and advisory.

- **Value metric.** Per month for a capped bundle (hours, deliverables or response time).
- **When it wins.** The customer needs you on call and the work is ongoing and hard to
  finish once. Good for services after a first project proves the value.
- **Watch out.** Buyers rarely commit to a monthly cost before they have seen one result.
  Sell a first one-off, then convert to a retainer. Do not lead with it in a cold sprint.
- **Jersey and regulated note.** Retainers suit firms that already trust a supplier. Trust
  is what you do not yet have on Day 5. Earn it with a one-off, then propose the retainer.

## The rule of thumb for this week

Speed of the first real yes beats theoretical lifetime value. Rank by how fast a Jersey
regulated buyer can pay you:

1. One-off build fee (fastest, fits a capital budget).
2. Pay-for-access plus build-in-partnership (fast for software, funds the build).
3. Retainer (needs trust you have not earned yet).
4. Subscription (slowest, needs recurring-cost sign-off).

Pick the highest one that fits your value metric. Hourly billing is not on this list on
purpose: it caps your income at the clock, invites haggling, and tells a regulated buyer
nothing about the outcome. Do not use it.
