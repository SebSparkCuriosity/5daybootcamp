# Red flags: the phrases that end deals in regulated Jersey sectors

This is the reference the Regulator Check leans on. It is not legal advice. It
is a working list of the claims that get a founder into trouble in front of a
finance, trust, fund admin, law or accountancy buyer, and what to write instead.
The rule underneath all of it: say only what you can prove, and hedge the rest
honestly.

## Why this matters more here than anywhere else

In an unregulated market, an overclaim costs you a bit of credibility. In
Jersey's regulated sectors it can cost you the deal on the spot, because your
buyer is themselves regulated. When you tell a compliance officer "guaranteed
returns", you have just handed them a reason to say no, and a reason they can
defend to their board. Underclaiming and proving it beats overclaiming and
hoping.

## The three verdicts

**SOURCED (green).** You can point to a real, checkable source. Your own logged
pilot result in CHANGELOG.md. A named public report with a date. A client figure
you have written permission to cite. If the source is real, keep the claim and
record where it came from.

**SOFTEN (amber).** True in spirit, not provable as worded. Rewrite it into
something you can stand behind. The pattern: swap the hard promise for the
mechanism or the design intent, or attach the one real data point you do have
and name it.

**RED.** Cannot be supported, cannot be softened into something meaningful. Cut
it. Superlatives about your market position and any guarantee of a financial
outcome are almost always red.

## The phrases, and the fix

**Guarantees of return, yield or profit.** "Guaranteed 8% returns",
"risk-free yield", "assured profit". These are the most dangerous words in the
list for a finance audience, and in many contexts they are regulated territory
in their own right. Almost always RED. Never promise a financial return you
cannot contractually deliver. Replace with the mechanism ("automates the
reconciliation that currently takes your team two days") not the outcome.

**"Risk-free", "no risk", "zero-risk", "100% safe".** Nothing is. RED for a
regulated buyer. Replace with the specific safeguard you actually have ("every
change is logged and reversible", "human sign-off before anything sends").

**"Regulated", "compliant", "compliance-ready", "approved", "certified",
"accredited".** Only claim these if they are literally true and you can name the
body and the registration. "JFSC-regulated" when you are not is a serious
misstatement. If you mean "built to be auditable", say that: "designed for
auditability, with a full changelog", not "compliant".

**"Bank-grade" / "military-grade" security.** Marketing shorthand with no fixed
meaning. If you rely on a provider's security (for example Supabase, Vercel),
cite the provider's posture, not a grade you invented. Soften to the specific:
"data held in [provider], encrypted in transit and at rest".

**Superlatives about market position.** "The best in Jersey", "the leading",
"the only", "#1", "fastest onboarding on the island". Unless you have a survey
or a dated ranking that says so, this is RED. Soften to a specific, provable
difference: "onboarding in one week, fixed price" beats "the fastest
onboarding".

**Unsourced numbers and percentages.** "Cut costs by 40%", "10x faster", "saves
20 hours a week". Amber unless sourced. If the number comes from one pilot, say
so and name it: "in our first pilot with [named client type], processing time
fell from X to Y". If you have no pilot, drop the number and describe the
mechanism.

**Comparatives.** "Cheaper than", "faster than", "outperforms". These invite the
question "than what, measured how?". Amber. Either name the comparison and the
measure, or cut the comparison.

**Ship dates and turnaround on pre-orders and proposals (hardware and
services).** "Ships in March", "delivered in 48 hours". Only state a date you can
hit. A missed date on a pre-order page taking real payment intent is the fastest
way to a chargeback and a complaint. Soften to a committed window you control.

## A quick self-test before you ship

Read each claim and ask: if the prospect emailed back "prove it", could I, today,
without inventing anything? If yes, it is SOURCED. If I could rewrite it into
something I could prove, SOFTEN. If neither, it is RED and it comes out.
